# E0 — Vocabulary Headroom Check (2026-10-25)

**Question.** With a frozen Grounding DINO-T on LAE-80C, is there room for vocabulary selection to help? And how much of the oracle gain reported by arXiv 2601.22164 comes from LAE-80C's disjoint per-source label spaces (H7) rather than per-image class relevance?

**This is an oracle diagnostic, not a method comparison.** Only the `full` condition is deployable.

## Findings
1. **H1 supported: large headroom.** The per-image GT vocabulary raises AP50@900 from 0.015 to 0.140 (×9.6, standard) and from 0.028 to 0.140 (×5.0, source-aware). AR@900 rises from 0.124 to 0.467. Both CIs exclude 0 by a wide margin. This replicates the *direction* of 2601.22164 with standard COCO metrics.
2. **H7: the label-space artifact is real but small.**
   - Under source-aware evaluation, the full vocabulary's AP50 roughly doubles (0.015 → 0.028). Of its 180,000 predictions, only 44,362 fall in the image's source label space: about 75% are spent on classes that are not annotated in that image's source.
   - But the oracle gap shrinks only from 0.126 to 0.112. **About 11% of the gap** is due to how standard evaluation treats the disjoint label spaces; about 89% remains.
   - Knowing the right source label space (~21 classes) gives only +0.003 (n.s.) under source-aware evaluation.
   - By its pre-registered criterion H7 holds (0.112 lies below the standard-protocol CI [0.119, 0.187]), **but the share is small.**
   - **Conclusion:** the prior study's interpretation (too many overlapping classes confuse the detector) is largely confirmed. Standard evaluation of LAE-80C additionally inflates the full-vocabulary penalty.
3. **Which classes matter, not how many.**
   - A random vocabulary of the same small size (2.2 classes) is *no better* than the full vocabulary (standard −0.008, n.s.; source-aware −0.021, CI excludes 0). Fewer tokens alone do not help.
   - So a selector that drops present classes can easily do *worse* than the full vocabulary. **Vocabulary recall is the critical property.**
4. **Within-source confusion dominates.**
   - With the ~21 classes of the correct source, AP50 is 0.031 vs. 0.140 for the ~2 classes actually present.
   - Per source (Table 3, source-aware): DIOR 0.060 → 0.064 → 0.259 (full → source oracle → GT oracle); FAIR 0.011 → 0.018 → 0.117; xView 0.013 → 0.012 → 0.085.
   - Coarse "domain-level" selection is not enough. Selection has to reach per-image granularity (a few classes).
5. **Small objects benefit most in recall.** R50 for small objects goes from 0.106 to 0.450 (medium 0.186 → 0.694, large 0.379 → 0.916). Under the full vocabulary many objects are missed or mislabelled.
6. **Detector ceiling.**
   - Even with the oracle vocabulary, AP50@900 is only 0.140.
   - **18 of 75 classes stay at ~0 AP50** even with the oracle vocabulary:
     - fine-grained vehicles: Pickup Truck, Utility Truck, Passenger Car, Cargo Car, Truck Tractor, Tractor;
     - attribute classes: working chimney, unworking chimney, unworking condensing tower;
     - basketball court, tennis court, Intersection, Locomotive, Front loader/Bulldozer, Ground Grader, Aircraft Hangar, Construction Site, Tower.

     No vocabulary selector can fix these. They bound what selection can achieve with GDINO-T at this resolution.
7. **Efficiency.**
   - Small vocabularies cut median end-to-end latency from 418 to ~333 ms (−20%) and the forward pass from 380 to ~297 ms; peak memory 2,188 → ~1,900 MB.
   - **Implication for H6:** at an oracle-like vocabulary size, a selector is latency-neutral only if it costs ≲ 85 ms per image on this GPU.
   - Absolute GDINO-T latency (~0.4 s/image) is high for this GPU. It is identical across conditions, so comparisons are fair; the cause (HF implementation, fp32) is to be profiled in M12.

## Caveats
- 200 dev images. Per-class AP for many classes rests on 1–2 images; the largest per-class gains (stadium, soccer ball field, Hut/Tent, …) are anecdotal.
- **Bootstrap:** the point estimates sit near the lower end of the percentile intervals (e.g. +0.126 in [+0.119, +0.187]), so the resampling distribution is shifted. A plausible cause is that categories drop out of resamples, which changes the per-class average.
  - Bias diagnostics and a "basic" interval were added to the evaluator afterwards. Re-scoring E0 (`evaluate_run.py` on the RTX 5060, where the predictions live) will add them.
  - The H1 conclusion does not depend on the interval method (the effect is ~10× its distance from 0). The H7 and random-control intervals should be read with this in mind.
- Single resolution (800/1333) with 59% small objects. Tiling could change the ceiling; if tested, it applies to every condition.
- Other processes held 1.7 GB of GPU memory at the start. This does not affect accuracy, but latency numbers should be confirmed on an idle GPU before they are reported in a paper.
- The HF implementation of Grounding DINO is assumed equivalent to the official one (risk 16).

## Decision (M0 gates)
- **H1 holds**, so build deployable selectors (M4) and test H2.
- Design requirements derived from E0, without test data:
  - (a) high **vocabulary recall at small budgets** (2–5 classes);
  - (b) report the **vocabulary-recall vs. AP** relationship, because a dropped present class is costly;
  - (c) use budgets fine-grained at the small end (e.g. k ∈ {1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 12, 20, 40, 80});
  - (d) keep selector cost ≲ 85 ms/image for a latency win;
  - (e) report both evaluation protocols.
- **Interpretation risk for the paper:** gains from any selector are capped by the oracle (≈ 0.140 AP50@900 on this sample) and by the 18 classes the detector cannot find.
